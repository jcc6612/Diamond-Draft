# DIAMOND DRAFT: THE 162–0 CHALLENGE
## Official Product Bible & Vision Constitution (V2)

---

## Section 1 — Vision Statement

### What Is Diamond Draft?
Diamond Draft is a **baseball-history roguelike** about opening sealed vaults of real MLB history, assembling impossible cross-era rosters, completing collectible baseball stories, and living with the legends you leave behind.

Every Franchise + Era roll opens a one-time door into a real chapter of baseball—from the 1890s Boston Beaneaters and 1920s Yankees to the 1950s Brooklyn Dodgers and 1990s Seattle Mariners. You inspect archival scouting dossiers of real major leaguers, draft one player onto your floodlit diamond or off-field dugout/bullpen, and permanently seal that franchise-era chapter for the rest of the run. Once your roster is locked, you test your creation against 162 games of baseball variance and October drama—chasing championships, Dynasty trophies, collectible stories, and the mythical 162–0 season.

The core emotional loop is **"One more run."** A complete Quick Draft takes 10 minutes, creates agonizing draft-room tradeoffs, and leaves behind a distinct historical narrative every single time.

### What Is Diamond Draft NOT?
- **Not a spreadsheet or stats database:** It must never feel like Baseball Reference, FanGraphs, or an analytics dashboard. Stats serve storytelling; they never replace it.
- **Not a front-office GM simulator:** It is not *Out of the Park Baseball*. There are no salary tables, arbitration hearings, minor-league promotion menus, or 200-hour franchise spreadsheets.
- **Not a ratings-driven fantasy or arcade card game:** OVR, Overall Rating, Team OVR, Legend Score, and Draft Score are **permanently banned from the UI**. Players evaluate real baseball history—era, awards, tenure, chemistry, and stories—never a single meta number.
- **Not a gacha or pay-to-win collector:** There are no synthetic players, no fictional fillers, no duplicate pack-opening loops, and no artificial legend caps.

## Section 2 — Core Design Pillars

Every future mechanic, UI update, or mode must reinforce these five permanent pillars:

1. **Historical Discovery**
   Every rolled Franchise + Era should feel like opening a museum drawer. Players must encounter both inner-circle icons and forgotten cult heroes, postseason specialists, and *"I forgot he played there"* stints—always 100% real MLB players with era-accurate club names and stories.
2. **Meaningful Sacrifice (Roads Not Taken)**
   Every pick is irreversible. Drafting one player permanently seals that Franchise + Era chapter for the run. The legends you leave behind are not discarded UI clutter—they are a core emotional mechanic that gives every decision real weight.
3. **Baseball Storytelling (Named Stories & Synergies)**
   The game is not about clicking the highest-impact legend in a vacuum; it is about building the coolest baseball story. Completing cross-era relationships (*Murderers' Row*, *Refuse to Lose*, *The Big Unit Battery*, *Big Red Machine*) creates genuine reasons to choose narrative over automatic star clicks without ever nerfing legends.
4. **Dynasty Collection & Legacy**
   Single 10-minute runs feed a permanent career. Players return to fill their Championship Trophy Room, collect rare Named Stories, log unforgettable Roads Not Taken sacrifices, and compete on shared Daily Challenge seeds.
5. **Black Diamond Prestige**
   The visual presentation must communicate quiet luxury and Cooperstown reverence—matte obsidian, brushed chrome, Championship Gold, stitch crimson, and a realistic top-down ballpark—instantly separating Diamond Draft from generic sports dashboards and neon mobile games.

## Section 3 — Historical Vault Philosophy

Empirical playtesting of the 43-pool, 1,144-player Seed Vault proved a foundational truth: **replayability comes from meaningful choices and memorable chapters, not raw player volume.** Across 20 simulated Quick Draft runs (140 total picks), rational play drafted only 65 unique players—proving that adding uncurated pre-1960 depth does not improve the draft room as much as curating iconic eras and rewarding stories.

Accordingly, Diamond Draft adopts a two-tier historical horizon:

### 1890s–1950s = Iconic Eras
- **Philosophy:** Curate baseball's most legendary, culturally resonant pre-expansion chapters (e.g., *1890s Boston Beaneaters*, *1900s Pirates & Cubs*, *1910s Red Sox*, *1920s Yankees & Giants*, *1930s Cubs*, *1940s Cardinals*, *1950s Brooklyn Dodgers*).
- **Why:** Pre-1960 baseball has fewer teams, thinner relief-pitching depth, and starker talent cliffs. Focusing on iconic chapters keeps vintage rolls electric, recognizable, and rich in Cooperstown lore rather than diluting the draft room with obscure dead-ball filler.

### 1960s–Present = Complete Baseball Universe
- **Philosophy:** Full, expansive coverage across all 30 modern MLB franchises and every expansion wave (1961–1962, 1969, 1977, 1993, 1998) through the 2020s—powered out of the box by the Seed Vault and expandable to 20,000+ real players via SABR Lahman CSV/JSON compilation.
- **Why:** From 1960 onward, dedicated bullpens, expansion-club identities, televised postseason folklore, and living fan memory make deep 25–75 player pools come alive with cult heroes and forgotten stints.

### Future Vault Modes (Roadmap)
- **Iconic Eras Only Mode:** A curated, high-density experience featuring only baseball's most famous franchise chapters—ideal for fast, high-drama Quick Drafts and balanced decade distribution.
- **Complete Historical Vault Mode:** The full compiled SABR Lahman universe (300+ Franchise-Era pools across 1890s–2020s) for deep-history obsessives and Full Team / Franchise / Decade purists.

## Section 4 — Roads Not Taken

**Roads Not Taken is the emotional center of Diamond Draft.**

In most sports draft games, unpicked players vanish the instant you click "Draft." In Diamond Draft, every Franchise + Era roll is a one-time door. The moment you pick **Wilcy Moore** out of the *1920s New York Yankees* to fill your last pitching slot—or intentionally pick **Dan Wilson** out of the *1990s Seattle Mariners* to complete *The Big Unit Battery*—the vault slams shut on **Babe Ruth**, **Lou Gehrig**, or **Ken Griffey Jr.** for the rest of the run.

### Why It Matters
- **It creates real draft-room tension without artificial caps:** You don't need a salary cap to feel pressure when every pick permanently locks out a chapter of baseball history.
- **It turns every run into a personal anecdote:** Players don't remember generic 135-win rosters; they remember *"the run where I passed on Ken Griffey Jr. for Dan Wilson and won the World Series."*
- **It elevates presentation:** Retired chapters are framed as **Sealed Vault Entries**, **Archived Legends**, and **Lost Baseball Timelines**—stamped in crimson `SEALED` typography—never as a trash bin of discarded picks.

## Section 5 — Named Stories & Synergies

The 20-run gameplay audit uncovered Diamond Draft's defining design challenge: when a pool has a clear mathematical #1 star (`32 of 43` pools), rational players click the legend every time—leaving cult heroes and role players untouched.

**The solution is never to nerf legends or cap Hall of Famers.** Ruth, Griffey, Pedro, and Kershaw must always feel titanic. Instead, **Named Stories** create a parallel, narrative-first axis of value—turning baseball history into collectible stories.

### The Three Story Tiers
1. **Tier 1 — Legendary Stories (Rare · High Prestige & Payoff)**
   Iconic, goosebump-inducing historical reunions: *Murderers' Row*, *The Core Four*, *Big Red Machine*, *Boys of Summer*, *Refuse to Lose*, *Pirates Immortals*, *The Big Unit Battery*, *2001 Co-MVP Twin Aces*, *Manny & Papi*, *Swingin' A's Dynasty*.
2. **Tier 2 — Franchise Stories (Medium · Club Identity)**
   Multi-era club lineages and expansion bonds: *Fenway Century*, *Braves Big Three*, *Bronx Bashers*, *Harvey's Wallbangers*, *Olympic Stadium Golden Core*, *Atlanta Three-Era Crown*, *Emerald City Torch*, *Blake Street Bombers*, *1998 Expansion Twins*, *1969 Expansion Brothers*.
3. **Tier 3 — Baseball Stories (Common · Dynamic Gameplay Variety)**
   Classic on-field baseball partnerships—both named batteries/duos and universal dynamic archetypes that work across any Lahman import: *Clubhouse Battery* (same-club P+C), *Double Play Duo* (same-era SS+2B), *Heart of the Order* (30-HR sluggers), *Ace & Closer Lock*, *Speed & Defense*.

### Mandatory Story Presentation Order
Every Named Story is designed in this exact priority order:
1. **Name** (evocative baseball lore)
2. **Story Text** (1–2 sentences of real historical narrative)
3. **Milestone Popup** (Black Diamond Chrome Title Card—*"You discovered a piece of baseball history,"* never an arcade achievement)
4. **Roads Not Taken Interaction** (explicitly logging any Hall of Famer passed on to complete the story)
5. **Gameplay Bonus** (meaningful chemistry and October clutch lift—hidden behind narrative labels, never raw OVR math)

### Hidden Future Synergies (Anticipation Loop)
Drafting an opener (e.g., Ken Griffey Jr. or Randy Johnson) unlocks **mystery teasers** (`☐ Refuse to Lose`, `☐ The Big Unit Battery`) *without* spoiling who completes them. When a later roll contains the completing player, their dossier lights up with a Championship Gold **`★ Story Available`** tag—making choosing Dan Wilson over Ken Griffey Jr. genuinely thrilling without making Dan Wilson a better individual player than Griffey.

## Section 6 — Black Diamond Identity

Diamond Draft's visual identity is **Luxury Baseball Vault**: Cooperstown's plaque gallery filtered through obsidian, brushed metal, and night-game stadium lighting.

### Logo Philosophy
The **Black Diamond Sports Emblem** fuses three unmistakable cues into one razor-sharp silhouette: a faceted obsidian gemstone, a dark-striped baseball infield with home plate and mound, and crimson baseball stitches along the upper shoulders.
- **Full Lockup (`DIAMOND DRAFT`):** Reserved for the Home Hero vault card and app icon.
- **Cropped Diamond Mark:** Used for the top navigation bar and browser favicon for crisp legibility at small sizes.

### Official Color Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Base Canvas** | Obsidian Matte | `#080A0D` | Primary backdrop and vault depth |
| **Elevated Surface** | Vault Graphite | `#101319` | Workspace panels and modals |
| **Card Surface** | Smoked Charcoal | `#151922` | Dossiers, dugout slots, sealed vault cards |
| **Chrome Accent** | Brushed Chrome | `#C7D0DE` | Hairline bevels, metallic wordmarks, metadata |
| **Prestige Accent** | Championship Gold | `#D9A441` | Hall of Fame foil, Legendary Stories, Story Available badges |
| **Tension Accent** | Stitch Crimson | `#D62828` | `SEALED` vault stamps, Roads Not Taken sacrifices |
| **Success Accent** | Ballpark Emerald | `#3E9E72` | Completed Baseball Stories, World Series championships |

Team colors remain strictly **contextual accents** (top border rule, subtle era-header glow, active field pin rim)—never flooding the obsidian canvas.

### Typography & Presentation Goals
- **Display Headlines:** `Barlow Condensed` (bold, uppercase, wide-tracked chrome metallic finish).
- **Narrative & Story Titles:** `Instrument Serif` italic (editorial gravitas for Milestone Title Cards and pull quotes).
- **Body & Dossiers:** `Inter` (clean, effortless scanability).
- **Archival Data:** `JetBrains Mono` (restrained era averages, years, and `SEALED` stamps).
- **Performance Rule:** Always fast, lightweight, and instant. Use restrained `180–320ms` mechanical micro-transitions (vault latch, chrome sweep)—never heavy particle systems, 3D WebGL bloat, or slow arcade pop-ups.

## Section 7 — 12-Month Roadmap

### Must Build (Months 1–4 · Core Loop to 50-Hour Replayability)
1. **Expand Named Stories Library (50 → 100+ Stories):** Cover all 30 franchises with Tier 1 Legendary and Tier 2 Franchise stories, plus deeper Roads Not Taken sacrifice recognition.
2. **Decade-Balanced Roll Weighting:** Shift roll weighting to balance decades more evenly so 1980s–2000s no longer consume ~59% of Quick Draft rolls and vintage eras shine regularly.
3. **Dynasty Progression & Story Collection Book:** Dedicated Dynasty Story Codex showing discovered vs. undiscovered stories (`X / 100`), rarest Roads Not Taken sacrifices, and permanent legacy milestones.
4. **Quick Draft Win-Band Calibration:** Tighten simulation scaling so typical strong Quick Draft rosters land in a suspenseful `105–125` win window (making `135+` truly historic and `162–0` mythical) instead of averaging `139` wins.

### Should Build (Months 5–8 · Meta Depth & Polish)
5. **Optional Run Contracts (Pre-Draft Quests):** Opt-in historical challenges (*Underdogs / Zero-HOF*, *Expansion Era Only*, *1980s Throwback*, *Battery Brothers*) awarding Dynasty Trophies without restricting standard play.
6. **Black Diamond Phase 2 Presentation:** Full-season championship ring/plaque celebration card, richer collectible dossier tier styling (Legends vs. HOF vs. Franchise Icons vs. Cult Heroes), and shareable end-of-run "Dynasty Poster" export.
7. **Iconic Eras Only vs. Complete Historical Vault Toggle:** Clean mode switch once a user loads a compiled SABR Lahman database.

### Could Build (Months 9–12 · Community & Expansion)
8. **Daily Challenge Leaderboards / Seed Comparison:** Compare your roster, completed stories, and Roads Not Taken against friends on the exact same `YYYYMMDD` seed.
9. **Negro Leagues Historical Chapters:** Honor SABR Lahman's Negro Leagues integration with dedicated iconic chapters and cross-era Legendary Stories.
10. **Custom Vault / Era Packs:** Shareable JSON era packs curated by baseball fans.

### Do Not Build (Explicitly Rejected)
- **Visible OVR / Player Ratings / Draft Grades**
- **Legend Caps, Salary Caps, or Legend Nerfs**
- **Pitch-by-Pitch / Play-by-Play Simulation Management**
- **Trades, Free Agency, Contracts, or Multi-Year GM Spreadsheets**
- **Gacha Pack Economies, Microtransactions, or Synthetic/Fictional Players**
- **Heavy 3D Graphics or Arcade Particle Effects**

## Section 8 — The Diamond Draft Test

Before any new feature, screen, mode, or tweak is approved for Diamond Draft, it must pass one non-negotiable question:

> **Does this make choosing between two real baseball players more agonizing, more storied, or more memorable?**

- If **yes**—and it preserves speed, zero visible ratings, and the Black Diamond Vault identity—**build it.**
- If **no**—even if it sounds clever, technically impressive, or standard in other sports games—**reject it.**

Diamond Draft wins not by doing 50 things like every other baseball game, but by doing 5 things better than anyone else.
